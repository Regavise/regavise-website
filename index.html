THE CHALLENGE

The costliest regulatory mistake can happen long before submission.

For early-stage medical-device teams, regulatory strategy begins with intended use, design decisions, testing plans, evidence needs, and quality systems—not with the final submission.

01

Fragmented information

Regulations, guidance, product classifications, and historical FDA decisions are spread across different sources and formats.

02

Decisions made too late

Gaps discovered late in development may require additional tests, redesign, or repeated studies—consuming time and capital.

03
?✓

Answers without judgment

Generic AI can summarize information. Regulatory work also demands source traceability, device context, uncertainty, and expert oversight.

THE OPPORTUNITY

Make regulatory knowledge more actionable earlier—especially for innovators who cannot maintain large in-house regulatory teams.

Our proposed approach →

DEVELOPMENT ROADMAP

A focused start.
A broader vision.

The first technical priority is a 510(k)-focused, source-grounded retrieval system. Later stages depend on testing, regulatory validation, and development milestones.

01
AVAILABLE ON THIS SITE

Public FDA evidence explorer

Working browser-side connection to openFDA 510(k) clearance and device classification data. A narrow but demonstrable first integration.

02
RESEARCH OBJECTIVE

510(k) retrieval-augmented generation pilot

Index selected FDA sources, compare embeddings and hybrid retrieval, test citation validity, and benchmark regulatory questions. Targeted undergraduate research; not presented as completed.

03
PLANNED

Expert-guided regulatory workflows

Connect device context to source-linked development plans, evidence needs, documentation checks, and human review.

04
FUTURE DIRECTION

Beyond 510(k)

Evaluate expansion to De Novo, HDE, PMA, and wider lifecycle support after the initial approach is validated.
